# RAG Chunks Assessment — Data Mining for Multimodal Retrieval Quality

## Overview

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems depend heavily on the quality of the document chunks that are retrieved and passed to the language model.

We investigated chunk quality as a **data mining problem** rather than treating retrieval performance as a single black-box metric.

The study uses the OHR-Bench evaluation framework containing 8,498 question-answer pairs across seven domains and evaluates retrieval using BM25 with top-2 chunk retrieval.

## The Challenge

> Can we use unsupervised data mining techniques to identify naturally occurring patterns in document chunks, distinguish high- and low-quality content, and guide targeted improvements to RAG retrieval performance?

Poor chunk quality can cause a chain of problems in a RAG system.

Low-quality chunks may fail to match relevant queries during retrieval, while poorly structured retrieved content can provide degraded context to the language model.

The problem becomes particularly difficult for complex content such as:

* Tables
* Mathematical formulas
* Multi-column layouts
* OCR-generated content
* Structured documents

## Methodology

We followed this pipeline:

1. **We collected** chunk-level evaluation data from OHR-Bench.
2. **We prepared** the dataset and associated query/retrieval context.
3. **We engineered** a 15-dimensional feature representation.
4. **We standardized** the numerical features.
5. **We selected** the optimal number of clusters using quality-aware K-selection.
6. **We applied** K-means clustering.
7. **We profiled** the resulting clusters statistically.
8. **We assigned** quality tiers according to LCS performance.
9. **We identified** characteristics associated with low-performing chunks.
10. **We enhanced** tables, mathematical formulas, and structural boundaries in the POOR tier.
11. **We re-ran** retrieval using the enhanced corpus.
12. **We compared** LCS performance before and after enhancement.
13. **We validated** improvements statistically.
14. **We analyzed** both improvements and potential degradation across clusters.
