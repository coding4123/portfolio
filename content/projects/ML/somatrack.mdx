# SomaTrack — Predicting Study-Related Physical Ailments

## Overview

**SomaTrack** is a machine learning project focused on predicting study-related physical discomfort among university students.

We built the system using survey data covering factors such as:

* Study habits
* Posture
* Screen usage
* Break frequency
* Hydration
* Study environment
* Seating type
* Sleep
* Exercise
* Other ergonomic and lifestyle conditions

Using these characteristics, We designed the system to estimate the user's overall physical discomfort risk and provide condition-specific predictions for:

* Back pain
* Neck strain
* Eye strain
* Tension headaches

The project combines **supervised machine learning, rule-based scoring, exploratory analysis, and personalized recommendations** to transform survey responses into an understandable risk assessment.

## The Challenge

> How can we use study habits and ergonomic factors to predict the risk of physical discomfort among university students while also identifying the factors that contribute most to that risk?

The challenge is not simply to build a classifier.

We needed the system to consider multiple behavioral, lifestyle, and environmental factors simultaneously and transform them into a meaningful assessment.

SomaTrack therefore combines machine learning predictions with additional rule-based analysis to provide both **risk estimates and contextual explanations**.

## Methodology

We followed this **SomaTrack workflow**, connecting data collection, preprocessing, machine learning prediction, rule-based scoring, and personalized recommendations.

1. **We collected** study and lifestyle data through a guided questionnaire covering study habits, posture, screen usage, break frequency, hydration, and other ergonomic factors.
2. **We preprocessed** questionnaire responses into the feature format expected by the trained Scikit-learn pipelines to ensure consistent preprocessing between training and prediction.
3. **We engineered and normalized** features by transforming and normalizing numerical and categorical variables (posture, seat type, lighting, study location) into features compatible with the trained pipelines.
4. **We applied** supervised machine learning by passing processed features into trained Scikit-learn pipeline models to estimate the user's overall physical discomfort risk based on behavioral, lifestyle, and ergonomic inputs.
5. **We ran** condition-specific prediction models in addition to the overall prediction, using dedicated models for back pain, neck strain, eye strain, and tension headaches.
6. **We calculated** prediction probabilities and converted them into percentage-based risk estimates for each discomfort category to aid interpretation.
7. **We combined** ML predictions with rule-based scoring functions (e.g., `score_back_pain()`) to evaluate specific ergonomic and behavioral factors and provide interpretable context.
8. **We generated** a structured assessment combining prediction probabilities and rule-based scores, including overall pain level, per-condition risks, summaries, and likely contributing factors for the user.
9. **We generated** personalized recommendations by translating the strongest risk areas into concise, actionable advice such as posture adjustments, break scheduling, hydration, and lighting improvements.
10. **We presented** the results through the React frontend with visual risk indicators, summaries, contributing factors, personalized recommendations, and an optional Pomodoro-style study helper.
