# Urban Sprawl Analysis — Multi-Decadal Satellite Analysis

## Overview

We worked on **Urban Sprawl Analysis**, a geospatial machine learning project that investigates long-term urban expansion across four Algerian cities:

- **Algiers** — coastal
- **Oran** — coastal
- **Sétif** — plateau
- **Ghardaïa** — Saharan

The analysis covers four temporal periods:

- **2000**
- **2010**
- **2020**
- **2024**

We trained a shared machine learning model using data collected across all cities and epochs, then applied it consistently to each city and year.

The resulting classification maps were used to calculate urban growth and sprawl metrics, allowing different geographic contexts and temporal growth patterns to be compared using a common methodology.

## The Challenge

> How can We use satellite imagery and supervised machine learning to consistently map and measure urban expansion across different Algerian geographic environments and multiple decades?

Urban expansion is not uniform across cities.

Coastal cities, plateau environments, and Saharan landscapes can have substantially different spectral characteristics, making built-up land difficult to distinguish consistently from vegetation, bare soil, and other land-cover types.

The challenge therefore extends beyond producing a single urban map. The workflow needed to provide:

- Consistent processing across different Landsat generations.
- Reliable built-up/non-built-up classification.
- Comparable results across cities and years.
- Reduction of classification noise and small false patches.
- Meaningful metrics for interpreting the spatial form of urban growth.

## Methodology

We followed this complete workflow across 12 stages:

1. **We defined** the study areas by establishing geographic boundaries for Algiers, Oran, Sétif, and Ghardaïa across four epochs: 2000, 2010, 2020, and 2024.
2. **We acquired and preprocessed** Landsat imagery by collecting Landsat 5 TM and Landsat 8/9 OLWe surface reflectance imagery, applying spatial and temporal filtering, and removing clouds and invalid pixels using QA masks.
3. **We generated** standardized annual composites by creating annual median composites for each city and epoch, then standardizing Landsat bands across sensor generations into a common feature structure.
4. **We engineered** spectral features by calculating NDBI, BUI, and NDVWe and combining them with relevant Landsat spectral bands to characterize built-up and non-built-up surfaces.
5. **We constructed** the training dataset by using GHSL to generate built-up/non-built-up labels and extract the corresponding spectral and index features from valid pixels across all cities and epochs.
6. **We cleaned and prepared** the data by removing invalid values, balancing the classes, and standardizing the features using StandardScaler.
7. **We trained** the global XGBoost model using the combined training data from all four cities and all four temporal epochs.
8. **We classified** built-up areas by applying the trained model to each city/year composite to generate built-up and non-built-up classification maps.
9. **We post-processed** classification maps by applying morphological opening and closing to reduce noise, remove patches below the minimum size threshold, and apply temporal smoothing to maintain consistent urban expansion.
10. **We calculated** urban growth metrics by measuring built-up area and percentage built-up area for each city and epoch, then computing fragmentation, growth rate, total growth, compactness-related measures, and mean distance from the city center.
11. **We compared** urban expansion by analyzing classification results and sprawl metrics across the four cities and four temporal periods to identify differences in urban growth patterns.
12. **We visualized and presented** the results by generating classification maps, time-series plots, comparison charts, summary tables, and the interactive dashboard for exploring the results.

This staged framework ensured the methodology remained consistent across cities and time periods while producing comparable urban-growth indicators from a common remote-sensing and machine-learning workflow.
