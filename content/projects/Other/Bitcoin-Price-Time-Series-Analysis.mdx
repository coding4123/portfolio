# Bitcoin Time Series — Analysis & Forecasting

## Overview

I investigated Bitcoin and other time-series datasets through statistical modelling and forecasting techniques.

The primary analysis used historical Bitcoin daily price data. I covered data preprocessing, exploratory analysis, stationarity testing, transformation, model identification, parameter estimation, residual diagnostics, volatility analysis, and forecasting.

In addition to the Bitcoin analysis, I used supporting experiments with simulated AR, MA, and ARMA processes to strengthen my understanding of autocorrelation, partial autocorrelation, and model identification.

## The Challenge

> How can I model Bitcoin's highly non-stationary and volatile price behaviour using classical time-series methods?

Bitcoin prices exhibit strong trends and changing variance over time, making direct modelling of the raw price series inappropriate for many classical statistical techniques.

I needed to transform the data appropriately, identify a suitable time-series structure, select statistically justified models, and account for volatility that changes over time.

## Methodology

1. **Data Preparation & Exploration**  I prepared the historical Bitcoin data and explored its overall trend, fluctuations, and changing volatility.

2. **Variance Stabilization**  I applied a logarithmic transformation to reduce the effect of increasing variance and obtain a more stable series.

3. **Stationarity Analysis**  I used **ADF** and **KPSS** tests to determine whether the transformed series was stationary.

4. **Differencing** When necessary, I applied differencing to remove remaining trends and achieve stationarity.

5. **Model Identification**  I analyzed **ACF/PACF** patterns to identify potential ARIMA structures for the stationary series.

6. **ARIMA Model Comparison** I tested multiple ARIMA configurations rather than assuming a single structure would best represent the data, then compared them using **AIC/BIC** to select the most appropriate model.

7. **Model Diagnostics** I examined the selected model's residuals to verify that the temporal patterns had been adequately captured and that no significant structure remained.

8. **Volatility Analysis**  I tested the residuals for **ARCH effects** to determine whether volatility changed over time and whether ARIMA alone was sufficient.

9. **GARCH Modelling**  I fitted GARCH-family models to capture the conditional volatility identified in the residuals.

10. **Forecasting**  I used the selected models to generate forecasts and prediction intervals.

11. **Forecast Evaluation**  I compared the forecasts with observed values using forecasting accuracy measures to assess model performance.

12. **Interpretation**  I combined the results from the mean and volatility models to understand Bitcoin's price dynamics, forecasting behaviour, and changing market volatility.
